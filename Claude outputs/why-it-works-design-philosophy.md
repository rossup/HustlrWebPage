# Exhibit

*A design philosophy where every claim arrives with its evidence.*

Exhibit treats persuasion as display, not assertion. A statement is never left alone on the page: beside it, in a quiet lit case, sits the object that proves it. The composition divides into two worlds, a calm index of short promises and a single illuminated vitrine where one piece of evidence is shown at a time. The eye moves from claim to proof and back, and that crossing is the whole argument. Each alignment is set with painstaking care so the relationship between a line in the index and the object in the case feels inevitable.

Form is architectural and spare. The index is a column of even, generous rows separated by hairlines, each row the same height, the same rhythm, like labels in a museum drawer. The vitrine is one deep, softly glowing plane with a single artefact at its centre and nothing competing for attention around it. There are no cards for the sake of cards and no decoration without a job. The whole is meticulously crafted so that restraint reads as confidence.

Colour works like gallery lighting. A deep forest-dark ground holds everything. Pale mineral type carries the index. The evidence glows in its own native colours, so it looks lifted from the world rather than drawn for the page. One warm signal colour marks the item currently on display, and nothing else. The palette is calibrated with the patience of someone lighting a room for an opening night.

Rhythm is a slow rotation. The case changes on its own, unhurried, like a turning plinth, until a visitor reaches out and chooses. Once chosen, it stays. Transitions are short, soft cross-fades, never slides or spins, because the evidence matters more than the motion. Scale moves in deliberate steps: confident headings, whispered monospaced indices, one small precise caption under each exhibit. Every size is chosen with master-level restraint.

Text is spare and testable. Each promise is a few plain words a person could check for themselves, and each caption names exactly what the exhibit is. Nothing is claimed that the case cannot show. The finished piece should feel like the product of deep expertise and countless refinements, every gap, fade and baseline resolved, so that trust seems to build on its own.
